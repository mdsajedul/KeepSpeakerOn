The problem

Sound familiar?

Many speakers switch themselves off after a few minutes of silence to save power. Bluetooth speakers, soundbars, studio monitors and powered PC speakers all do it.

It turns itself off

After 5–30 minutes without sound, the speaker powers down and you have to switch it back on.

The first second is cut off

Videos, songs and notifications start clipped while the speaker wakes up from standby.

You miss what matters

Call alerts and the first words in meetings and online classes never reach you.

The fix

Set it once. Forget it forever.

KeepSpeakerOn sits in the system tray and plays a signal far too quiet to hear, so your speaker never sees silence and never goes to sleep.

Inaudible

Very quiet white noise (−60 to −90 dB) or a 10–15 Hz tone below the range of human hearing.

Plays alongside everything

Music, YouTube, games and calls sound exactly as before. Nothing is mixed out or lowered.

Follows your speaker

Switch your default output and KeepSpeakerOn moves to the new device within a second.

Reconnects by itself

It waits through Bluetooth disconnects and resumes as soon as your speaker comes back.

Sleep-aware

Pauses when your PC sleeps and resumes on wake. It never keeps your PC awake.

Featherweight

About 0% CPU and 2 MB of memory. No admin rights, no internet access, no telemetry.

How it works

Up and running in 30 seconds

  1. Download

    Get KeepSpeakerOn.exe and put it in a folder you'll keep, like C:\Tools.

  2. Run it

    Double-click the exe. A green speaker icon appears next to the clock. That's it, it's working.

  3. Done, for good

    It starts with Windows automatically, so it's back in the tray after every restart. Turn that off any time from the tray menu.

See a blue "Windows protected your PC" screen? That's SmartScreen, which warns about new apps that aren't code-signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway. How to check the file is genuine

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop my Bluetooth speaker from turning off automatically on Windows?

Most Bluetooth speakers switch off after 5–30 minutes without sound. Run KeepSpeakerOn and leave it in the system tray. It keeps sending a signal too quiet to hear, so the speaker stays on as long as your PC is awake.

Why does my speaker cut off the first second of sound?

When there's no audio, many speakers and soundbars enter power-saving standby and take a moment to wake up when sound starts, so the start of notifications, videos and songs is clipped. KeepSpeakerOn keeps the speaker active, so nothing is cut off.

Can I hear the signal KeepSpeakerOn plays?

No. The default is white noise at −70 dB, far below normal listening levels. You can choose −80 or −90 dB, or a 10–15 Hz tone, which is below the range of human hearing.

Does KeepSpeakerOn drain my speaker's battery?

The signal itself uses a negligible amount of power. It plays far below hearing level, so the speaker's amplifier does almost no work to reproduce it.

What does use battery is the speaker staying switched on. Auto-off exists to save power, and preventing it is exactly what KeepSpeakerOn does. While it runs, the speaker's Bluetooth radio, processor and amplifier remain powered, and the battery drains at the speaker's own idle rate. That's usually slower than playing music, but faster than being switched off.

Plugged-in speakers, such as soundbars, studio monitors and powered PC speakers, are unaffected. For battery-powered speakers, keep them on charge at your desk, or right-click the tray icon and choose Pause when you won't be listening for a while. When your PC sleeps or shuts down, KeepSpeakerOn stops as well.

Why not just play a silent MP3 on loop?

A silent file is digital silence, which most speakers detect as "no signal", so they go to sleep anyway. KeepSpeakerOn sends a real but inaudible signal, follows device changes, and survives sleep and disconnects.

Is KeepSpeakerOn safe? Why does Windows show a SmartScreen warning?

KeepSpeakerOn is open source under the MIT license, doesn't connect to the internet and needs no admin rights. Windows SmartScreen warns because the exe isn't code-signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.

Does KeepSpeakerOn work on Windows 10, Windows 11, macOS or Linux?

It works on Windows 10 (64-bit or 32-bit) and Windows 11. macOS and Linux aren't supported.
