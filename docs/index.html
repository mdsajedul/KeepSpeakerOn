Stop your Bluetooth speaker turning off automatically

SpeakerAwake is a free, open-source Windows app that keeps Bluetooth, USB, HDMI and powered speakers out of auto-standby with a signal you can't hear.

Download for Windows View on GitHub 64 KB · No installer · Windows 10 & 11

The problem

Many speakers switch themselves off after a few minutes of silence. Then the first second of every notification, video or song gets cut off while the speaker wakes up, or you have to walk over and press the power button again. It happens with Bluetooth speakers, soundbars, studio monitors and powered PC speakers.

How SpeakerAwake fixes it

It runs quietly in the system tray and plays a signal far too quiet to hear, so the speaker never sees silence and never goes to sleep.

  • InaudibleVery quiet white noise (−60 to −90 dB) or a 10–15 Hz sub-audible tone.
  • Plays alongside everythingMusic, YouTube, games and calls sound exactly as before.
  • Follows your speakerConnect your speaker and SpeakerAwake moves to it automatically.
  • Reconnects by itselfIt waits through disconnects and resumes when the speaker returns.
  • Sleep-awarePauses when the PC sleeps and resumes on wake. It never keeps your PC awake.
  • FeatherweightAbout 0% CPU, about 2 MB of RAM, no admin rights, no telemetry.

Works with

Any speaker that turns off after a period of silence, on any output Windows can play to: Bluetooth speakers (Awei, JBL, Anker Soundcore, Sony, Tribit, Xiaomi, Bose, Marshall and others), Bluetooth headphones, soundbars and AV receivers over HDMI or optical, powered speakers and studio monitors with auto-standby, and USB DACs. See tested devices and limitations.

How to use it

  1. Download SpeakerAwake.exe and put it in a permanent folder.
  2. Double-click it. A green speaker icon appears in the system tray.
  3. Right-click the icon and tick Start with Windows.

Blue "Windows protected your PC" screen? That's SmartScreen, which warns about new apps that aren't code-signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway. How to check the file is genuine.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop my Bluetooth speaker from turning off automatically on Windows?

Most Bluetooth speakers switch off after 5–30 minutes without sound. Run SpeakerAwake and leave it in the system tray. It keeps sending a signal too quiet to hear, so the speaker stays on as long as your PC is awake.

Why does my speaker cut off the first second of sound?

When there's no audio, many speakers and soundbars enter power-saving standby and take a moment to wake up when sound starts, so the start of notifications, videos and songs is clipped. SpeakerAwake keeps the speaker active, so nothing is cut off.

Can I hear the signal SpeakerAwake plays?

No. The default is white noise at −70 dB, far below normal listening levels. You can choose −80 or −90 dB, or a 10–15 Hz tone, which is below the range of human hearing.

Why not just play a silent MP3 on loop?

A silent file is digital silence, which most speakers detect as "no signal", so they go to sleep anyway. SpeakerAwake sends a real but inaudible signal, follows device changes, and survives sleep and disconnects.

Is SpeakerAwake safe? Why does Windows show a SmartScreen warning?

SpeakerAwake is open source under the MIT license, doesn't connect to the internet and needs no admin rights. Windows SmartScreen warns because the exe isn't code-signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.

Does SpeakerAwake work on Windows 10, Windows 11, macOS or Linux?

It works on Windows 10 (64-bit or 32-bit) and Windows 11. macOS and Linux aren't supported.
